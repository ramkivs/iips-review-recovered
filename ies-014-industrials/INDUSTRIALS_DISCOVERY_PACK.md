## 2. Sector economics

How Industrials businesses create and earn value:
- **Primary revenue model:** capital equipment and machinery, aerospace & defense, engineering & construction, transportation/logistics, electrical/industrial components, diversified industrials.
- **Cost structure:** manufacturing/COGS, labor, R&D/engineering, capex; operating leverage to volumes.
- **Value chain:** design/engineering → manufacturing → distribution → OEM/end-market installation → aftermarket/service (recurring).

## 3. Market structure

- **Segments:** Capital Goods (machinery, equipment), Aerospace & Defense, Transportation (rail, trucking, logistics, airlines), Engineering & Construction (E&C), Electrical Equipment, Conglomerates/Diversified, Defense primes.
- **Business models:** OEM (original equipment manufacturer), Aftermarket/service (recurring), Project/EPC (lump-sum engineering), Distributor, Diversified portfolio.
- **Drivers:** capital-expenditure cycles, industrial production, infrastructure spend, defense budgets, aerospace order books, transport volumes.

## 4. Value drivers

- **Backlog / order book** — visibility into future revenue.
- **Book-to-bill** — demand vs shipments ratio.
- **Aftermarket / recurring revenue** — high-margin, sticky service stream.
- **Pricing power / mix** — premium products, services.
- **Cost efficiency / operating leverage** — margin expansion with volume.
- **Capital allocation** — M&A, buybacks, capex discipline.
- **What destroys value:** order cancellations, project overruns, margin compression, over-leverage, cyclical demand shocks, defense/aerospace program risk.

## 5. Regulatory environment

- Defense/export controls, aerospace certification (safety), environmental/emissions, trade/tariff exposure, government procurement, safety standards.

## 6. Lifecycle

- Industry lifecycle stage: **mature, cyclical** (capital-goods cycle); defense/aerospace more visible (long backlogs).
- Cyclicality: **high** for capital goods/transport; **moderate** for defense/aerospace.
- Structural trends: reshoring/nearshoring, automation, electrification, infrastructure investment, services/aftermarket mix shift.

## 8. Proposed methodology dimensions (candidate)

- **Quality** — aftermarket mix, cost position, program execution.
- **Growth** — backlog, orders, volume growth.
- **Risk** — cyclicality, project/program risk, leverage.
- **Valuation** — P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, PEG.
- **Capital efficiency** — ROCE, FCF yield, capital return.
- **Moat** — aftermarket/services lock-in, technology, scale, defense franchises.
