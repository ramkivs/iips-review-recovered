## 1. Purpose

The conceptual foundation for the Utilities metric library and methodology — answers *why* metrics are chosen, not just *what* they are, grounded in the reusable Regulated Infrastructure Common Principles.

## 2. What creates durable investment value

- **Rate-base growth that compounds** — regulatory asset base expansion.
- **Constructive regulatory relationships** — allowed-return stability, low regulatory lag.
- **Investment quality** — well-approved, recoverable capex (grid modernization, transition).
- **Operating efficiency** — O&M control, reliability.
- **Balance-sheet resilience** — strong FFO/Debt, investment-grade.
- **Transition positioning** — grid/electrification investment with recovery.

## 3. What destroys value

- **Adverse rate cases** — disallowances, low allowed ROE.
- **Regulatory lag** — unrecovered cost inflation.
- **Capex overruns** — cost overruns not recovered in rate base.
- **Stranded assets** — transition-related asset write-downs.
- **Over-leverage** — balance-sheet stress.
- **Operational/reliability failures** — penalties, service failures.

## 4. Leading vs lagging indicators

| Indicator | Type |
|---|---|
| Rate base growth trend | Leading (future earnings) |
| Allowed-return / rate-case outcome trend | Leading (returns trajectory) |
| Regulatory lag trend | Leading (recovery efficiency) |
| Capex program / transition investment | Leading (future rate base) |
| Demand / usage trend | Leading (volume trajectory) |
| FFO/Debt trend | Leading (balance-sheet resilience) |
| Current revenue / ROE / earnings | Lagging |
| Dividend yield / valuation | Lagging |

Methodology weights **rate-base growth + regulatory quality + investment quality + balance-sheet resilience** (leading) and uses ROE/earnings to confirm.

## 5. Universal vs utilities-specific principles

**Universal (regulated infrastructure common):** regulatory economics, allowed-return models, capital recovery, rate-base growth, regulatory lag, reliability, investment cycles, defensive valuation (per Regulated Infrastructure Common Principles).

**Utilities-specific (core):**
- Rate base growth.
- Allowed ROE / regulatory return.
- Regulatory relationship / rate-case quality.
- O&M efficiency + reliability.
- Transition / grid investment.

## 6. How Utilities Sector Profiles should be expressed

```text
Sector: Utilities
  Sub-sectors: Regulated Electric, Gas Distribution, Water, IPP/Merchant, Multi-utility
  Regulatory basis: rate base × allowed return (regulated); merchant (competitive)
  Applicable Metrics (universal regulated-infrastructure + utilities set)
  Applicable Overrides (adverse rate case, regulatory lag, capex overrun, stranded asset, leverage breach)
  Applicable Calibration (regulated vs merchant; segment thresholds)
  Regulated-return emphasis
  Defensive / low-cyclicality profile
```

Lives in the **methodology**, not the platform.

## 7. How regulated-return economics should be treated

- Regulated-return is the **primary quality/valuation basis** (rate base × allowed return).
- **Regulated vs merchant** is a **calibration** consideration (merchant = higher risk, commodity/price exposure), not scoring-logic change.
- **Regulatory quality** (constructive vs adversarial, regulatory lag) is a leading quality signal.

## 8. How rate-base growth and investment should be treated

- **Rate-base growth** is the primary growth driver — a leading signal.
- **Investment quality** (recoverable, pre-approved capex) matters more than gross capex.
- Sustaining vs growth capex distinction (per Regulated Infrastructure Common Principles).

## 9. How balance-sheet resilience should be treated

- **FFO/Debt** and investment-grade status are core quality signals.
- Utilities are capital-intensive; low leverage and steady cash flows matter.
- Balance-sheet strength is a leading resilience indicator.

## 10. How the energy transition should be treated

- Transition/grid investment is a **leading growth + quality** signal (if recoverable).
- Stranded-asset/transition risk is a **risk** consideration.
- Expressed via calibration/segments, not scoring-logic change.

## 11. The Utilities economic moat

- **Regulated monopoly / rate-base moat** — exclusive service territory, recoverable returns.
- **Infrastructure** — long-life, essential assets.
- **Stable regulated cash flows** — defensive, low cyclicality.
- **Regulatory relationships** — constructive regulator = enduring advantage.

Maps to the Universal Investment Ontology's **Moat** dimension — CSIP-compatible via ontology registration.

## 12. Invariant principles

1. **Rate-base quality > gross size** — recoverable, approved investment.
2. **Regulatory relationship is foundational** — constructive > adversarial.
3. **Regulated-return economics drive value** — rate base × allowed return.
4. **Balance-sheet resilience > growth** — FFO/Debt discipline.
5. **Transition is real, but must be recoverable.**
6. **Deterministic, replayable, explainable** — platform-neutral.
