## 2. Sector economics

How Utilities businesses create and earn value:
- **Primary revenue model:** regulated rate-base × allowed return (power/gas/water distribution + transmission), plus unregulated/merchant segments.
- **Cost structure:** capital-intensive infrastructure (generation, transmission, distribution), regulated cost recovery, O&M.
- **Value chain:** Generation → Transmission → Distribution → Retail; gas midstream/distribution; water/sewerage.

## 3. Market structure

- **Segments:** Regulated Electric Utilities (generation/transmission/distribution), Gas Utilities (distribution/LNG), Water Utilities, Independent Power Producers (IPP, merchant), Multi-utilities.
- **Business models:** Regulated-return (rate base), Merchant/competitive (IPP), Vertically-integrated.
- **Drivers:** rate cases (allowed ROE, rate base), electricity/gas demand, infrastructure investment, energy transition (grid/electrification), regulatory environment.

## 4. Value drivers

- **Rate base growth** — regulatory asset base expansion.
- **Allowed return / ROE** — regulated return on equity.
- **Regulatory stability** — constructive vs adverse rate cases.
- **Infrastructure investment** — grid modernization, renewables integration.
- **Demand / usage** — electricity/gas/water volumes.
- **Operating efficiency** — O&M cost control, reliability.
- **What destroys value:** adverse rate cases, capex overruns, stranded assets, over-leverage, regulatory disallowances, operational failures.

## 5. Regulatory environment

- Rate regulation (ROE, rate base, cost recovery) — the defining feature.
- Public utility commissions (PUCs), federal/state regulators.
- Environmental (emissions, grid), reliability, safety standards.
- Energy transition mandates (renewables, electrification, grid investment).

## 6. Lifecycle

- Industry lifecycle stage: **mature, defensive**, low cyclicality.
- Long-duration capital projects; steady, regulated cash flows.
- Structural trends: energy transition (grid, electrification, renewables), distributed generation, climate resilience.

## 10. Regulatory Framework & Rate-Case Lifecycle

Unlike most sectors, Utilities derive significant enterprise value from regulatory outcomes rather than competitive pricing. Key concepts (likely core methodology principles, not implementation details):

- **Rate-case frequency** — how often returns are reset via rate cases.
- **Allowed-return methodology** — how the permitted ROE/return is set (cost-of-capital, DCF, comparable earnings).
- **Regulatory lag** — the time between cost changes and rate-case recovery (earnings lag).
- **Capital recovery** — how capex enters the rate base and is recovered (depreciation, CWIP, tracker mechanisms).
- **Constructive vs adversarial regulators** — regulatory relationship quality.
- **Transmission/distribution investment approval** — pre-approval of capital programs.
- **Tariff adjustment mechanisms** — fuel/energy adjusters, decoupling, formula rates (reduce lag).
