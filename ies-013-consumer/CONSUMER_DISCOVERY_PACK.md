## 2. Sector economics

How Consumer businesses create and earn value:
- **Primary revenue model:** branded consumer products (food/beverage, personal care, household), durable/electrical goods, apparel, retail, restaurants/quick-service.
- **Cost structure:** COGS (raw materials), brand/advertising investment, distribution, labor; scale economies.
- **Value chain:** R&D/branding → sourcing/manufacturing → distribution/retail → consumer demand.

## 3. Market structure

- **Segments:** Staples (food/beverage, household, personal care — defensive), Discretionary (apparel, durables, autos, restaurants — cyclical), Luxury (premium brands).
- **Business models:** Branded manufacturer, Retailer, Restaurant/Franchise, DTC (direct-to-consumer), Multi-brand.
- **Drivers:** consumer demand, pricing power, brand strength, distribution, penetration/expansion, disposable income.

## 4. Value drivers

- **Pricing power** — ability to raise prices without losing volume (brand moat).
- **Brand strength** — consumer loyalty, premium positioning.
- **Demand durability** — repeat/staple demand vs discretionary.
- **Distribution quality** — shelf space, channels, DTC, global reach.
- **Margin resilience** — input-cost pass-through, operating leverage.
- **Capital allocation** — buybacks, dividends, reinvestment, M&A discipline.
- **What destroys value:** brand erosion, category disruption, input-cost squeeze without pricing power, channel loss, over-leverage, failed M&A.

## 5. Regulatory environment

- Consumer protection/safety, food/labeling, advertising standards, trade/tariff exposure, sustainability/environmental (packaging, sourcing).

## 6. Lifecycle

- Industry lifecycle stage: **mature** (staples) with **growth** segments (premium, health/wellness, emerging-market).
- Cyclicality: staples **defensive**, discretionary **cyclical**.
- Structural trends: e-commerce/DTC shift, premiumization, health/wellness, sustainability, emerging-market growth.

## 8. Proposed methodology dimensions (candidate)

- **Quality** — brand strength, pricing power, demand durability.
- **Growth** — organic volume/price growth, expansion.
- **Risk** — input-cost, category disruption, cyclicality (discretionary).

## 10. Consumer Demand & Brand Flywheel

Unlike many other sectors, long-term value creation in Consumer businesses is often driven by an interconnected **flywheel** rather than a single operating metric. Key concepts (likely foundational methodology principles):

- **Pricing power vs volume growth** — the trade-off between rate and volume.
- **Premiumization** — shifting mix toward higher-price, higher-margin products.
- **Repeat purchase behavior** — recurring consumer demand.
- **Customer lifetime value (LTV)** — long-term value per customer.
- **Brand equity** — consumer awareness/preference → pricing power + loyalty.
- **Distribution reach** — shelf space, channels, DTC, global footprint.
- **Innovation pipeline** — new products sustain growth + relevance.
- **Category leadership** — share leadership in the category.
- **Market-share durability** — sustained/stable share over time.

This flywheel (brand → loyalty → pricing power → reinvestment → brand) is a core Consumer methodology principle.
