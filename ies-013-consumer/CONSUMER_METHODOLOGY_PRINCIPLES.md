## 1. Purpose

The conceptual foundation for the Consumer metric library and methodology — answers *why* metrics are chosen, not just *what* they are, grounded in the reusable Intangible Competitive Advantage Common Principles.

## 2. What creates durable investment value

- **Brand equity + pricing power** — the ability to raise prices without losing volume.
- **Demand durability** — repeat/staple demand (staples) vs discretionary.
- **Distribution reach** — shelf space, channels, DTC, global footprint.
- **Customer loyalty / LTV** — recurring demand, low acquisition cost.
- **Innovation pipeline** — sustained relevance + premiumization.
- **Margin resilience** — input-cost pass-through + operating leverage.
- **Efficient capital allocation** — buybacks, dividends, reinvestment discipline.

## 3. What destroys value

- **Brand erosion / category disruption.**
- **Input-cost squeeze without pricing power.**
- **Channel loss / distribution decline.**
- **Over-leverage / value-destructive M&A.**
- **Share loss / market-share erosion.**
- **Innovation failure** — relevance loss.

## 4. Leading vs lagging indicators

| Indicator | Type |
|---|---|
| Pricing power / volume mix trend | Leading (pricing + demand) |
| Market-share trend | Leading (category position) |
| Repeat-purchase / loyalty trend | Leading (demand durability) |
| Distribution / channel mix trend | Leading (access to demand) |
| Innovation pipeline | Leading (future growth) |
| Input-cost pass-through trend | Leading (margin resilience) |
| Current revenue / margin / ROIC | Lagging |
| FCF yield / valuation | Lagging |

Methodology weights **pricing power + brand/loyalty + demand durability + distribution + innovation + margin resilience** (leading) and uses profitability/ROIC to confirm.

## 5. Universal vs consumer-specific principles

**Universal (intangible-asset common):** brand equity, customer loyalty, network effects, distribution, switching costs, innovation, pricing power, marketing efficiency, intangible durability (per Intangible Competitive Advantage Common Principles).

**Consumer-specific (core):**
- Pricing power vs volume growth.
- Staple vs discretionary demand.
- Brand strength / loyalty.
- Distribution / channel mix.
- Margin resilience (input-cost pass-through).
- Capital return (buyback/dividend).

## 6. How Consumer Sector Profiles should be expressed

```text
Sector: Consumer
  Sub-sectors: Staples, Discretionary, Luxury
  Business models: Branded manufacturer, Retailer, Restaurant/Franchise, DTC, Multi-brand
  Applicable Metrics (universal + consumer set)
  Applicable Overrides (brand erosion, category disruption, input-cost squeeze, channel loss, leverage breach)
  Applicable Calibration (segment thresholds + weights; staples-defensive vs discretionary-cyclical)
  Brand / pricing-power emphasis
  Intangible-asset durability profile
```

Lives in the **methodology**, not the platform.

## 7. How pricing power and demand durability should be treated

- **Pricing power** (brand moat) is a foundational quality signal.
- **Demand durability** distinguishes defensive staples from cyclical discretionary.
- Staple vs discretionary is a **calibration** consideration, not scoring-logic change.

## 8. How the brand flywheel should be treated

- The brand → loyalty → pricing power → reinvestment → brand flywheel is the core value engine.
- Brand equity, repeat purchase, LTV, and market-share durability are leading quality signals.

## 9. How margin resilience should be treated

- **Input-cost pass-through** and operating leverage determine margin stability.
- Margin resilience is a leading quality signal; reflected in calibration (staples vs discretionary).

## 10. How capital allocation should be treated

- Buybacks, dividends, and reinvestment discipline are quality signals (capital return).
- Value-destructive M&A and over-leverage are risk signals.

## 11. The Consumer economic moat

- **Brand + pricing power** (intangible moat).
- **Distribution + scale.**
- **Customer loyalty / switching costs.**
- **Innovation / category leadership.**

Maps to the Universal Investment Ontology's **Moat** dimension — CSIP-compatible via ontology registration.

## 12. Invariant principles

1. **Brand + pricing power > gross size.**
2. **Demand durability is foundational** (staple vs discretionary).
3. **Distribution is access to demand.**
4. **Margin resilience > peak margin.**
5. **Capital discipline > growth.**
6. **Deterministic, replayable, explainable** — platform-neutral.
