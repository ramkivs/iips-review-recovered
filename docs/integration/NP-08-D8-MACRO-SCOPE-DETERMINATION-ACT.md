## 5. WHY THE DECISION RESOLVES THE DEFERRAL

The deferral was indeterminate by construction, and only an explicit act could resolve it.

1. **The deferral was expressly bounded.** The D8 scope act determined scope *"**for the initial D8 Intelligence target**"* — it did not purport to settle macro's standing in the governed D8 scope generally.

2. **The deferral was expressly non-terminal.** *"'DEFERRED' does NOT mean 'ABANDONED'."* Deferral therefore could not be read as either inclusion or exclusion; it suspended the question.

3. **The M-5 conditional had no determinate antecedent.** M-5 — *"Relief from D91 (authority D88) for macro, **if** macro remains within Intelligence scope"* — could not resolve while its predicate was unresolved.

4. **No act resolved it in the interim.** Verified across IRR `main` and IPD: no act stated macro was in scope, no act stated macro was out of scope (as a D8 scope determination), and no act resolved the deferral.

5. **Ramki has now decided.** Under the same Scope Determining Authority capacity that created the deferral, Ramki has explicitly selected **A — macro remains in D8 Intelligence scope**. The deferral is discharged; the conditional antecedent is now determinate.

> **The deferral is resolved by authority, not by inference.** No gap was filled by general engineering convention.
