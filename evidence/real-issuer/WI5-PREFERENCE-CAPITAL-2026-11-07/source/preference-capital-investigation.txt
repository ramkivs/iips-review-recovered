What this supports and what it does not
  Supported: at both reporting dates the audited consolidated statements present no preference share capital, the
  statement of changes in equity has no preference column, and the equity section reconciles exactly to equity share
  capital + other equity + non-controlling interests (482.32 + 21,992.69 = 22,475.01; + 19.69 = 22,494.70). A
  preference class large enough to matter would have had to appear somewhere in that decomposition.
  Not supported: a *declaration*. Nothing in the filing states that preference share capital is nil, that the Company
  has no preference shares, or that its share capital consists solely of equity. What exists is (a) a sentence about
  classes of EQUITY shares, under a heading scoped to equity shares, and (b) presentation that omits a preference
  component.

Why the earlier nil was withdrawn
  D-2 records a nil only where the source declares it, quoted, at the same period. Completeness arithmetic,
  presentation and a scoped sentence are inferences; converting them into NIL_BY_DISCLOSURE would make the platform
  assert something the filer never said. The distinction matters in the direction that hurts: an inference written as
  a declaration is indistinguishable, downstream, from a real one.
  The WI-3 precedent does not transfer. Infosys's accepted sentence reads "the Company has only one class of SHARES
  referred to as equity shares having a par value of ₹5/-" — one class of shares, named equity — and its
  capital-management note adds "…and has no debt". eClerx says "only one class of EQUITY shares", which leaves the
  existence of a preference class untouched.

Result
  No qualifying explicit nil disclosure for PREFERENCE_CAPITAL. The dataset cell is empty, the governed observation is
  MISSING / NOT_REPORTED, and no zero is substituted. The withdrawn declaration and the run that produced it are kept
  under runs/superseded/ and artifact/superseded/.
